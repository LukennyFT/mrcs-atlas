Intercollegiate MRCS · Part A

Three ways to revise the same syllabus

Same engine, same 298 topics, same saved progress — three genuinely different interfaces. Rate a topic in one and it shows up in the other two, so you can try each for a few days and keep whichever you actually open.

1 · Theatre
Dark · dense · desk

A console. Left icon rail, tabular topic rows with traffic-light buttons you can hit without opening anything, monospace numerals, day-counters showing how long since you last touched a topic.

  • Fixed left rail with a live countdown
  • Collapsible clinical-cluster accordions
  • Rate straight from the list, no expanding
  • Blueprint bars scaled to real mark counts

2 · Atlas
Light · serif · long-form

A textbook. Cream paper, Iowan/Palatino serif, oxblood accents, justified reading columns and numbered entries. Built for long sittings where you actually read rather than tick.

  • Four-step mastery ladder, not traffic lights
  • Unread → Read → Recalled → Mastered
  • Bookmarks (❧) and marginal notes
  • Editorial masthead with chapter tabs

3 · Ward Round
Near-black · lime · thumb-first

A phone app. Bottom tab bar, chunky rounded cards, big touch targets. Opens on a treemap where each section's tile is sized by marks at stake and coloured by how solid you are.

  • Blueprint heatmap as the home screen
  • Emoji confidence dial (😖 / 😐 / 💪)
  • Auto-generated "6 weakest, marks-weighted" queue

Where the content came from. Every section, cluster and mark count is taken from the Intercollegiate Candidate guide to the MRCS examination (July 2018) — Section 3 for the ten syllabus modules and Section 5 for the exact question allocation — cross-referenced against the ISCP Core Surgical Training (2017) syllabus, including the individual module pages (basic sciences, the clinical method, peri-operative care, basic surgical skills, critical care, paediatric surgical care, the dying patient and health promotion) and the Appendix 2 index of surgical conditions.

Running it. python3 server.py from the mrcs-app folder, then open http://localhost:8078. Everything is stored in this browser's localStorage — no account, no network, works on a plane.
